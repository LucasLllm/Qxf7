# Ask Wemotaci's council before any nurse

Write first to the **Conseil des Atikamekw de Wemotaci**, not to its nurses, the CISSS or Santé Québec. That is the opening move that gives QxF7 the best chance of a yes. In the same week, send learning-only emails to two places that need no Indigenous consent step: **Santé Québec Chaudière-Appalaches** (about Isle-aux-Grues) and **Vertiko Mobilité** (before anyone at Fumes mentions Manawan). Wemotaci is the best fit for a first conversation. It has the strongest documented need with no competitor: a fixed **Wednesday-morning blood-draw day**, a band-run health centre with named leaders, its own medical-transport team, and a single gravel road that failed in August 2026. No drone project is working there ([Wemotaci Santé](https://www.wemotaci.ca/sante); [Le Nouvelliste, 2026-08-03](https://www.lenouvelliste.ca/actualites/actualites-locales/2026/08/03/les-routes-forestieres-mises-a-mal-par-la-pluie-en-haute-mauricie-VXMAUMOXKNBLZI4LHXA7XEIYLY/)). The AFNQL Research Protocol says plainly that "the first letter should be sent to the band council" ([AFNQL Research Protocol, 2014](https://iddpnql.ca/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/protocole-recherche-en-web.pdf)). Going around the council to individual nurses is the fastest way to be refused or to give offence. Manawan is politically committed to Vertiko's CORRIDAIR, so it should be approached only through or alongside Vertiko, and only as a learner. The ethics line is clear enough to act on. Under TCPS2, asking staff to describe procedures they handle in their jobs does not make them research participants; asking for their personal opinions does ([TCPS2 2022, Ch. 2](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter2-chapitre2.html)). Get a written determination from your teacher, and from your school's ethics committee if in doubt, before the first interview. The outreach will also change the design report. Wemotaci is **91.5 km** straight-line, while your own model gives about **52 km** of winter range. The flagship route is therefore a summer or road-closure service unless a relay is added. Short island, river and roadless-coast hops (a few km up to about 43 km) are the only routes that are both documented-need and winter-feasible.

## Fourteen contacts in three waves, with Wemotaci and Isle-aux-Grues first

The contact list follows one rule from the governance research. For a band-run First Nations health centre, the **band council is the first door**, and the health director is the second, reached with the council's permission. For a Santé Québec CLSC, the **regional innovation office and the logistics or lab managers** are the first door. Clinic nurses come after either one. At Wemotaci and Manawan the clinic belongs to the community, not to Santé Québec. Manawan's health services have been band-run under a transfer agreement with Health Canada since 1994 ([manawan.com, Santé Mission](https://www.manawan.com/sante_mission), pre-2024). Wemotaci's centre operates under its band council with its own director ([Wemotaci Santé](https://www.wemotaci.ca/sante)). Indigenous Services Canada funds both but does not gatekeep either ([Open Canada grants record](https://rechercher.ouvert.canada.ca/grants/record/isc-sac,1910-2024-2025-Qrt4-0001805,current)). The Conseil de la Nation Atikamekw does not list health among its services, so it is a courtesy contact, not a permission step ([atikamekwsipi.com](https://www.atikamekwsipi.com/)).

Inside Santé Québec, the person who owns specimen transport is the regional establishment's **logistics or procurement function, with Optilab as the main client**. That is not the medical director and not national headquarters. The clearest proof is a July 2026 tender by **Santé Québec Mauricie-et-Centre-du-Québec** for "transport inter-installation – RLS du Haut St-Maurice et RLS de Trois-Rivières", "principalement pour le service de laboratoire". ## Band councils hold the first door, and Manawan belongs to CORRIDAIR

### The protocol: council first, collective consent, OCAP®, results returned

The **AFNQL First Nations of Quebec and Labrador Research Protocol** (2014, 2nd ed.; companion guide 2019) is the governing document. Its scope is deliberately broad: "all research, evaluations, surveys, focus groups etc. involving First Nations individuals or led on First Nations territory" ([AFNQL PDF](https://iddpnql.ca/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/protocole-recherche-en-web.pdf)). It requires the following:

- **First contact:** a first letter to the band council.
- **Transparency on money:** disclose all funding sources and funders' roles.
- **Agreement:** a research agreement that builds in OCAP.
- **Consent:** collective consent before any research begins, plus individual consent.
- **Opting out:** the community or an individual may opt out at any time.
- **Results:** results must be validated and returned to the community.
- **Interpreters:** paid at "three to four times" French–English rates.

Whether it covers informal student needs-finding is **ambiguous**. The text never mentions students or customer discovery. The safe reading, and the one the governance research recommends, is to follow its procedure for the first contact and its spirit for everything after. The FNQLHSSC distributes the protocol but is an advisory body with no intake form. Do not describe a call to it as "approval" ([FNQLHSSC](https://cssspnql.com/en/?p=4857)).

**OCAP®** (Ownership, Control, Access, Possession) means communities "determine the purpose of research and development projects… which researchers or developers are involved, who gets to see and share the results." For drones specifically, it extends to listing every data stream collected ([Taylor 2023](https://cdnsciencepub.com/doi/10.1139/dsa-2023-0058); secondary source, so confirm the FNIGC wording before quoting). In practice this means four things:

- your notes belong to the community and are deleted on request;
- the community's name and data are not published without written agreement;
- no patient-level data, ever;
- a written summary goes back in French, with an Atikamekw version if you budget for translation.

Atikamekw is spoken by the whole population and French is the second language ([Quebec.ca](https://www.quebec.ca/gouvernement/portrait-quebec/premieres-nations-inuits/profil-des-nations/attikameks)). Offer a paid interpreter for any community-member or Elder session. Start the letter with **"Kwei"**, but don't overdo it.

### What succeeded and what failed elsewhere

The documented successes all started from **existing relationships**. UBC's drone project with Stellat'en First Nation rested on decades of relationship built largely by Dr John Pawlovich. It also relied on community liaisons, open houses, school visits, and community review of the interview guide. Its most-cited harm came when deliveries stopped at the end of Phase I: "When you bring stuff to our communities you're supposed to leave it, and you're not supposed to take it back" ([JoPM 2026;18:e82720](https://jopm.jmir.org/2026/1/e82720)). CORRIDAIR began when a Saint-Donat family-medicine group that provides telemedicine to Manawan noticed long medication delays ([Radio-Canada](https://ici.radio-canada.ca/espaces-autochtones/2009045/joliette-manawan-drone-medicament-hopital)). In other words, a clinician the community already trusted was the bridge. Taylor, Schroeder & Redvers (2025) interviewed First Nations people in the drone sector. Their advice is blunt: "the best path forward is a slow one"; "You can't pick a region and assume it speaks for everyone"; and "if you try and go in there and tell them what they need… you'll be kicked out a lot faster than you know" ([Drone Syst. Appl., 2025](https://cdnsciencepub.com/doi/10.1139/dsa-2024-0046)). Their participants also raised camera data as potential "new form of colonization", the community's say over its own airspace, and overflight of sacred sites.

For a team with no aircraft, the most important risk is the **"parachute project"** pattern: outsiders define the need, test on a community, then leave. The honest framing is "we are trying to learn whether and how this would help, on your terms." Do not say "we want to pilot our drone here."

### How to handle Manawan

Manawan's council signed a "résolution de soutien et de participation" for CORRIDAIR in September 2023. Chief Sipi Flamand said at the time: "La meilleure façon de le faire, c'est d'impliquer les personnes concernées… plutôt que de présupposer leurs réactions" ([Vertiko release](https://vertikomobilite.ca/communique-de-presse-mobilite-aerienne-au-service-de-la-communaute-atikamekw-de-manawan/)). Radio-Canada named the CISSS de Lanaudière as a partner and quoted its then deputy CEO, **Philippe Éthier**, who is now the establishment's PDG per its 2025–2028 plan ([Radio-Canada](https://ici.radio-canada.ca/espaces-autochtones/2009045/joliette-manawan-drone-medicament-hopital); [Plan d'établissement](https://www.cisss-lanaudiere.gouv.qc.ca/fileadmin/internet/cisss_lanaudiere/Documentation/Plan_d_etablissement/Plan_d_etablissement_2025-2028_VF_20251020.pdf)). Vertiko's own release does not mention the CISSS, so the health system's formal role is **unconfirmed**. Québec committed up to $2 M. No flights had been reported as of October 2026 (project doc `claude/qxf7-competitor-landscape.md`).

Approaching Manawan cold with a route of your own would put the council in an awkward position. It would also look like you were exploiting CORRIDAIR's delay, and it would burn a relationship with the one Quebec actor that has health-system and First Nation sponsors in place. The sequence is:

## Job-role facts stay informal; opinions make it research

TCPS2 draws the line by **what you ask**, not by who you are. People who provide information "in the ordinary course of their employment" about "organizations, policies, procedures" are **not participants**. People "asked for their personal opinions about organizations… are considered participants because they are themselves the focus of the research" ([TCPS2 Art. 2.1](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter2-chapitre2.html)). The official interpretations add that "TCPS does not provide for any exemption from REB review based on who conducts the research," and that opinions "outside the scope of their job roles" require review ([TCPS2 Interpretations: Scope](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/policy-politique_interpretations_scope-portee.html)). Two traps apply to you specifically:

- **Course work is covered.** TCPS2 explicitly includes "course-based research activities, the primary purpose of which is pedagogical."
- **The QA/QI exemption does not fit.** The Art. 2.5 exemption covers quality-improvement work done inside an organization, so outside students gathering needs cannot claim it.

**You stay on the informal-consultation side when all of these hold:**

- You talk with a handful of staff about how the work process works: routes, schedules, volumes, cut-off times, who decides.
- The purpose is an engineering design choice in a school report.
- Notes are attributed only by role and region, e.g. "a lab logistics manager, Chaudière-Appalaches".
- Nothing is published.
- No personal or patient information is collected.
- Nothing is recorded, or it is recorded only with explicit consent and deleted once notes are written.

**You cross into research, and need CÉR review first, if any of these happen:**

- You ask how people feel or what they think of Optilab or their employer.
- You run a structured interview series that you analyse thematically for findings.
- You plan to publish, enter a science fair, or present results as research.
- You record and transcribe for analysis.
- You recruit through a CISSS or observe clinical work. Santé Québec establishments then also require scientific, ethics and "convenance" review before authorizing anything ([Québec.ca](https://www.quebec.ca/sante/professionnels/encadrement-recherche-participants-humains/autorisation-projets-recherche-participants-humains-etablissement-sante-services-sociaux)).
- You collect community-level data about a First Nation that could affect its welfare (TCPS2 Ch. 9 and the AFNQL protocol).

Re-using informal notes later for a paper or grant is treated as secondary use and needs review then.

**When to go to the school ethics committee:** in week 1, with a half-page description asking for written confirmation that review is not required. The Cégep de Sherbrooke guide, which is typical, says that when in doubt "il est toujours préférable de consulter le comité éthique," and that the CÉR "a le pouvoir d'interdire ou d'interrompre une recherche" ([Cégep de Sherbrooke guide](https://cegepsherbrooke.qc.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/guide-etudiant-sur-lethique2019-1.pdf)). Your own school's policy may differ, so check it. In First Nations settings the job-role exemption carries less weight, because the AFNQL protocol is broader than TCPS2. There, apply Chapter 9 practice voluntarily: council engagement, reciprocity and returning results ([TCPS2 Ch. 9](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter9-chapitre9.html)).

One more boundary. Bill 96 makes French the norm for the Quebec administration's interactions ([Exemplarité de l'État](https://cdn-contenu.quebec.ca/cdn-contenu/adm/min/langue-francaise/fr/publications/modernisation/13_exemplarite-etat_napperon.pdf)). Establishment media policies route *media* requests to communications ([CISSS-BSL policy, 2016](https://www.cisss-bsl.gouv.qc.ca/sites/default/files/fichier/politique_des_relations_de_presse_-_pdg-po-001_1.pdf)). Say up front that yours is neither a media request nor research, and that you are asking people to describe their own work, not to speak for their employer. No current Santé Québec-wide policy on employees talking to students was found.

## Ask about the last shipment, never about the drone

The method comes from three practitioner sources. All three say the same thing: talk about their past behaviour, not your idea.

- **The Mom Test:** ask about specific past events, not opinions about the future. "Compliments are the fool's gold of customer learning." End by asking "who else should I talk to?" ([summary](https://mtlynch.io/book-reports/the-mom-test/)).
- **NIH I-Corps:** "Do not pitch your product or technology" ([NIH I-Corps](https://grants.nih.gov/sites/default/files/I-Corps_at_NIH_Dec8_Final_508.pdf)).
- **Stanford Biodesign:** turn what you hear into solution-neutral statements of the form "A way to [problem] in [population] in order to [outcome]" ([Biodesign guide](https://biodesignguide.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/Need-Statements-v2.pdf)).

The drone stays out of the conversation unless they ask. If they ask, answer honestly and briefly.

**Opening (2 minutes, every role):** say who you are; that this is a school design report and you are non-profit for now; that nothing is built and nothing is for sale; that it may become a venture later; that you want to understand how specimens or medications actually move today; that there will be no questions about patients; and that nothing is recorded without consent. **Closing:** "Who else should we talk to? May we send you a summary to correct?"

**A. Nurse at a remote clinic (dispensaire / centre de santé)**

| Question | Listen for |
|---|---|
| Walk me through the last time you sent a specimen to the lab: collection time, how it left, when the result came back. | The real timeline, handoffs and carrier |
| Tell me about the last specimen that was rejected, hemolysed, late or lost. What happened next for you and for the care plan? (Process only, no patient details.) | Frequency and cost of failure |
| When did a transport schedule last force you to change *when* you drew or tested something? | Hidden workarounds |
| What did you do the last time a result or medication was needed urgently after the regular transport had left? | Current fallback (medevac, patient travel, wait) |
| How many times did that happen last month? | Volume, which sizes the case |
| Do you spin or stabilise anything locally? Which tests run on point-of-care devices? | Whether time windows actually bind |
| Who decides how specimens travel from here, and who pays? | The real decision-maker |

**B. Optilab / lab logistics or pre-analytical manager**

| Question | Listen for |
|---|---|
| How are pickups from outlying sites scheduled today, and who carries them? | Carrier contracts and frequency |
| Which route or site gave you the most trouble in the last 3 months? What happened on the worst day? | A specific route to design for |
| Which stability or time-to-centrifugation limits actually bite, and for which tests? | Design constraints (Lanaudière's procedure gives 2 h for an unspun gel tube at 25 °C) ([LAB-PNT-027](https://www.cisss-lanaudiere.gouv.qc.ca/fileadmin/internet/cisss_lanaudiere/Professionnels/Biologie_medicale__laboratoires_/Biologie_medicale_Nord/LAB-PNT-027_Transport_des_echantillons_vers_le_laboratoire_du_CHDL_v6_01.pdf)) |
| Do you track rejections for "délai de transport inapproprié"? Can you share non-confidential aggregate counts? | Evidence that the MSSS measurement manual records this ([MSSS 2025-26](https://publications.msss.gouv.qc.ca/msss/fichiers/2025/25-922-01W.pdf)) |
| The last time you changed a route or carrier, who approved it and how long did it take? | The procurement path for any future pilot |
| What would any carrier have to meet: packaging, chain of custody, temperature, receiving hours? | Requirements for the design report |

**C. First Nation health director**

| Question | Listen for |
|---|---|
| What are the community's health priorities right now? (Let them lead; transport may not be one.) | Whether the problem matters to them at all |
| Tell me about recent times getting a lab result or medication to or from the community was a problem. What was done? | Concrete incidents (e.g., the August 2026 road closure) |
| How are decisions about new services or partners made here: council, health committee, Elders? | The consent path |
| What experiences, good or bad, has the community had with outside projects? | Trust history and red lines |
| If anything ever came of this, what would the community need to control or be involved in? | OCAP, local operators, data and airspace terms |
| Who else should we talk to, and what protocol should we follow? | The next door |

**D. Pharmacist (hospital or community) supplying remote clinics**

| Question | Listen for |
|---|---|
| How did the last urgent medication request from a remote site reach the patient, and how long did it take? | The baseline time |
| Which products are hardest to get there: cold-chain, controlled, short-dated? | Payload and thermal requirements |
| What stock do clinics keep because transport is unreliable, and what expires? | Waste, as a cost proxy |
| Which rules govern who may transport medications and how? | Regulatory constraints for the secondary use case |

Never ask "Would you use a drone?", "Is this a good idea?" or "How much would you pay?", and never ask about specific patients. A draft need statement to test by week 8: *"A way to shorten collection-to-analysis time for time-sensitive specimens drawn at nurse-run clinics in [region] in order to reduce rejected specimens and repeat draws."*

## Follow-ups double replies; warm introductions beat endorsement letters

There is **no peer-reviewed study of cold interview requests to Quebec nurses or managers**. The evidence below is transferable proxy evidence, and the source type is labelled for each finding.

| Finding | Source type | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Physician interview recruitment: 20% opted in on the first contact, ~46% after follow-up; interviews scheduled by email were nearly double those scheduled by phone ([Sydney study summary](https://pc4tg.com.au/recruiting-primary-care-physicians-qualitative-research-experiences-recommendations-childhood-cancer-survivorship-study/)) | Peer-reviewed (via secondary summary) | Expect about half of your yeses from follow-ups; schedule by email |
| A peer's pre-contact call: OR 1.39 (significant). Personalised package: OR 1.73. Sponsorship letter: OR 1.38 (**not significant**). Pre-notification letter: not significant. Cash: £10 OR 3.14 ([Pit et al. 2014, BMC Med Res Methodol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4059731)) | Peer-reviewed systematic review (authors call it preliminary and heterogeneous) | A warm intro beats a teacher letter for reply rate. The letter still matters for gatekeepers. Don't offer cash to public employees |
| People asking strangers for help overestimate how many people they must ask by ~50% ([Flynn & Lake](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/bitstream/handle/1813/75652/Bohns14_If_You_Need_Help_Just_Ask.pdf)) | Peer-reviewed | Make a direct, specific ask; it works more often than you expect |
| 12 M outreach emails: 8.5% replied; one follow-up +65.8%; 36–50-character subjects best; personalised body +32.7%; multiple contacts at one organization +93% ([Backlinko, 2019](https://backlinko.com/email-outreach-study)) | **Vendor** (Semrush-owned; correlational; sales context) | Use 3–4 touches, a personalised first line, and 2–3 separate contacts per organization (not CC'd) |
| No follow-up 4.1% vs 3–5 follow-ups 8.3%; research-based personalisation ~17–18% vs ~7–9%; lists under 50 contacts beat 1,000+ ([Woodpecker, 2026](https://woodpecker.co/blog/cold-email-statistics/)) | **Vendor** (it appears to recycle Backlinko's +65.8%, so it is not independent) | Keep the list small and hand-researched |
| Weekday sends beat weekends by ~2 points ([Backlinko](https://backlinko.com/email-outreach-study)); pooled day-of-week effects not significant in the clinician review ([Pit et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4059731)) | Vendor + peer-reviewed | Send Tuesday to Thursday; don't overthink it |
| "Vision, framing, weakness, pedestal, ask" request structure ([Mom Test](https://mtlynch.io/book-reports/the-mom-test/)) | Practitioner/expert | Name the specific thing you can't see from outside and why *they* can see it |

The tactics follow from those numbers. Keep the email body under about 150 words and attach the one-page *fiche* rather than explaining everything in the email. Open with one personal, verifiable detail, for example:

- the 2019 "centrale des transports" report;
- the Haut-Saint-Maurice transport tender;
- the APTS finding that urgent tests must be done within 120 minutes while transport can take up to 90 ([APTS, juin 2026](https://aptsq.com/media/rrahwdwh/bilan-de-sant%C3%A9-des-laboratoires-juin-2026.pdf)).

Offer 2–3 concrete slots, including early morning and evening, because nurses work shifts. Send from a school address if you have one; no study measures its effect, but it is a plausible credibility signal. The strongest pattern in the medical-drone origin stories is a **specific, quantified pain** brought to someone with operational authority. Aviant's NTNU students started from news about 10–15-hour drives for test samples ([MIT Professional Education](https://professional.mit.edu/news/articles/developing-drones-address-pandemic-related-challenges-scandinavia)). Lead with the route problem, not the aircraft.

### Things not to do

| Don't | Why |
|---|---|
| Contact Wemotaci or Manawan nurses before the band council | Breaches the AFNQL protocol's first rule. Nurses may be agency staff who cannot speak for the community |
| Pitch Manawan as a QxF7 route, or contact Manawan before Vertiko | Puts the council against its own 2023 resolution; looks opportunistic |
| Ask the CISSS about an Indigenous community's need before that community agrees | Speaking for the community is the relationship-ending mistake |
| Lead with the drone, send product-like renders, or ask "would you use it?" | Ends problem talk and invites polite fluff ([Mom Test](https://mtlynch.io/book-reports/the-mom-test/); [I-Corps](https://grants.nih.gov/sites/default/files/I-Corps_at_NIH_Dec8_Final_508.pdf)) |
| Promise pilots, dates, savings or service | Repeats the documented harm of services that end ([JoPM 2026](https://jopm.jmir.org/2026/1/e82720)) |
| Ask for, or record, patient information | Professional secrecy; it would also push you into research ethics territory |
| Use CISSS, Santé Québec, Optilab or First Nation names or logos in a way that implies partnership | Say plainly that no health organization has mandated you |
| Show up unannounced in a community, or bring tobacco or other gifts by assumption | Visits are by invitation only; gift protocols vary by nation. Ask a liaison |
| Pitch at FNQLHSSC community gatherings | They are wellness and governance forums; pitching damages credibility |
| Use the August 2026 closure or a death as a sales hook | Exploitative. Refer to transport conditions factually and only if they raise them |
| Write to the MSSS Bureau de l'innovation now | You are ineligible (not incorporated, no product, no lobby registration) |
| Send during community emergencies or respiratory-surge headlines | Defer immediately at any sign of overload |
| Claim the work is "not research" to a First Nation as though that settles it | The community decides how it classifies the work |

## Conclusion

The outreach problem is mostly about sequence, not persuasion. Every source points the same way. The communities with the worst access, Wemotaci and Manawan, are the ones where a premature or misdirected email does lasting damage. The Santé Québec side is reachable through published generic innovation addresses, and its staff can talk about their own procedures without triggering research ethics. So the low-risk, high-information move is to run both tracks in parallel: a patient, protocol-correct letter to the Wemotaci council, and fast, process-focused conversations with Chaudière-Appalaches logistics and the La Tuque lab. Neither track needs the drone in the conversation.

The bigger implication is for the aircraft itself. Your own winter model rules the flagship route out of year-round service. The only places with both documented need and winter range are short hops of a few km up to about 43 km across a ferry gap, a freeze-up or route-blanche closure, or a roadless coast. That suggests the cold-weather claim is best proven on an island before it is promised to an Atikamekw community. The most credible thing you can bring to Wemotaci's council in 2027 may be a winter season of flight data from somewhere else, together with a clear account of what you learned and gave back. A drone design is the less credible offer.
