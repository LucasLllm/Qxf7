# QxF7 Faces Few Rivals, One Close Neighbour

Nobody in Quebec, Canada or the rest of the world runs what QxF7 proposes: a scheduled, same-day lab-specimen service using a small all-electric fixed-wing VTOL rated to −30 °C, flying about 95 km to nurse-run clinics. That gap is real, but three facts change how QxF7 should present itself. **(a) CORRIDAIR, led by Vertiko Mobilité with Jaunt/AIRO, already holds the Manawan corridor politically.** It has a band-council resolution signed in Sept 2023, up to $2 M in MEIE funding, CISSS de Lanaudière as a partner, and a published 150 km BVLOS route from Joliette to Manawan through Saint-Michel-des-Saints, although no flights have been reported ([Vertiko](https://vertikomobilite.ca/communique-de-presse-mobilite-aerienne-au-service-de-la-communaute-atikamekw-de-manawan/); [CRIAQ](https://www.criaq.aero/en/corridair/)). **(b) The Jan 16, 2024 Delair DT46 flight of more than 150 km, run by Quebec's ECOGENE-21, took place near Toulouse, France, not in Quebec.** The aircraft circled for about 3 h at Delair's Labège test centre ([France 3 Occitanie](https://france3-regions.franceinfo.fr/occitanie/haute-garonne/toulouse/photos-un-drone-reussi-un-vol-de-livraison-de-medicaments-sur-plus-de-150-km-afin-d-aider-des-patients-isoles-2915880.html)). **(c) Flightory's Stork license covers personal, non-commercial use only and explicitly prohibits use by organizations, schools, universities and research institutions, including built aircraft** ([Flightory License](https://flightory.com/license/)). As things stand, a non-profit or school-linked QxF7 flying for clinics would breach that license. The closest real-world model is Aviant in Norway, a student-founded electric fixed-wing VTOL operator. It flew 120 km hospital blood-sample routes in 2021, but by 2026 it earns most of its revenue from consumer delivery, and its hospital-corridor trials are not scheduled until 2027–28 ([Shifter](https://www.shifter.no/nyheter/30-mill-i-kontrakter-og-usa-avtale-i-sikte-etter-ski-lansering/470044); [SINTEF](https://www.sintef.no/en/projects/2026/aero-aid-drones-more-effecient-norwegian-healthcare/)). The most defensible differentiator is −30 °C electric operation, which is also QxF7's biggest technical risk. No published operating limit for any electric fixed-wing VTOL goes below −20 °C, and the only peer-reviewed study found reports an unheated Li-ion UAV failing below about −25 °C ([Li et al. 2021](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/energy/v219y2021ics0360544220325883.html)).

## Bottom line: an empty niche, one occupied corridor, and a license problem

The landscape splits cleanly. Operators that actually fly medical routes do so over short distances: Volatus's Canary multirotor covers about 10–14 km in Alberta and Ontario, Wing and Matternet fly urban NHS hops of a few minutes, and Everdrone's AED drones work within about 8 km ([Skies Mag](https://skiesmag.com/features/volatus-aerospace-cutting-edge-bvlos/); [Apian](https://www.apian.health/swlp-stg); [Interhospi](https://interhospi.com/everdrone-expands-world-first-medical-emergency-drone-network-in-sweden/)). Long-range medical flights of 50–150 km exist mostly as trials: Aviant, Delair/ECOGENE-21, Delivrone in Normandy, CAELUS in Scotland and Falck in Greenland. Zipline's P1 is the exception, running routine operations in temperate and tropical climates ([MDPI Drones](https://www.mdpi.com/2504-446X/10/1/71); [Strathclyde](https://www.strath.ac.uk/whystrathclyde/news/2025/projectshowspotentialoffirstnationalmedicaldronesnetworkforscotland/)). The research found no documented routine, real-patient, long-range lab-specimen service flown through a sub-−20 °C winter between 2024 and 2026. That is the niche QxF7 names.

The three critical flags reshape strategy. On (a), Manawan → Saint-Michel-des-Saints should be offered to Vertiko and the Conseil des Atikamekw de Manawan as a collaboration, not announced as QxF7's own flagship. Wemotaci → La Tuque has no known competitor and is the cleaner first route. On (b), any claim in the Preliminary Design Report that a long-range medical drone has "already flown in Quebec" is wrong; no Quebec-flown long-range medical demonstration was found. On (c), the team must buy a commercial or institutional license from Flightory or move to a clean-sheet airframe before operating for clinics. Scaling the design does not appear to escape the restriction on derivatives. The history of the field also carries a lesson: technology rarely kills these programs. Moose Cree, CAELUS, Greenland, Nepal and Malawi all ended or stalled when pilot funding ran out and no health authority took over the operating cost ([D+C](https://www.dandc.eu/en/article/malawi-medical-drones-once-stregthened-healthcare-funding%20cuts); [DDC 2018](https://dronedeliverycanada.com/press-releases/drone-delivery-canada-signs-2-5-million-commercial-agreement-with-moose-cree-first-nation-to-deploy-ddcs-drone-delivery-platform/)).

## Our project: QxF7 in one paragraph

Fumes Technologies is Lucas Liu and Mario Pacheco Senda, two students in Quebec. It is a non-profit for now, may become for-profit later, and is preparing a Preliminary Design Report for teachers. QxF7 is a scaled-up Flightory Stork: a 3D-printed, fully electric quadplane with four lift motors, one pusher and a V-tail. It is still in planning and has not been built. The fixed requirements are a **750 g gross payload** (including UN3373 packaging and thermal protection), **about 95 km one-way range**, **operation down to −30 °C**, and range prioritised over speed, with **cruise of at least 70–80 km/h** to cope with northern winds. The primary mission is scheduled same-day lab-specimen runs, with medication delivery second, for small remote or seasonally isolated Quebec communities with nurse-run clinics. Illustrative, non-final routes are Manawan → Saint-Michel-des-Saints CLSC (about 70 km, the flagship), Wemotaci → La Tuque (about 91.5 km), and a test route between Isle-aux-Grues and Montmagny (about 8 km). The team claims four differentiators: low cost through 3D printing (estimated parts CA$1,700–3,550, compared with about US$12–17k for a DeltaQuad Pro), extreme-cold capability, a focus on small remote communities, and non-profit status. Aviant is the role model. All of these figures are the team's own targets and estimates, not measured results.

For reference, the stock Stork VTOL has a 1,620 mm span, 1.4–3.1 kg all-up weight, a 50–70 km/h cruise, 4S–6S Li-ion/LiPo packs and an ArduPilot configuration. Its manual gives **no endurance, range, payload or cold-weather figures** ([Stork VTOL manual](https://flightory.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/STORK-USER-MANUAL-VTOL.pdf)). A July–August 2026 ArduPilot Discourse thread asking for real-world Stork VTOL build data received no owner reports ([ArduPilot Discourse](https://discuss.ardupilot.org/t/proven-3d-printable-vtol-fixed-wing-project/144948)). QxF7's range, speed and cold targets therefore all go beyond anything documented for the base design.

## Quebec: CORRIDAIR owns Manawan; everyone else is a demo or a partner

No routine drone medical-delivery service operates in Quebec as of October 2026. Three efforts come close to QxF7's mission, and a ring of infrastructure and industry players could serve as partners.

### CORRIDAIR (Vertiko Mobilité + Jaunt/AIRO): same corridor, same community, much bigger aircraft

CORRIDAIR was announced on Sept 8, 2023 at Volaria in Mirabel. The Conseil des Atikamekw de Manawan signed a band-council resolution supporting and participating in a drone corridor for essential goods ([Vertiko](https://vertikomobilite.ca/communique-de-presse-mobilite-aerienne-au-service-de-la-communaute-atikamekw-de-manawan/)). The idea came from a Saint-Donat family-medicine group that provides telemedicine to Manawan. The route runs through Saint-Donat and **Saint-Michel-des-Saints**, flown at 200–300 ft and 150 km/h, taking about 1 h compared with about 3 h by car. As announced in 2023, testing was planned for spring 2024–2025, deliveries "during 2026", and air taxis in 2028–29 **[pre-2024]** ([Radio-Canada](https://ici.radio-canada.ca/espaces-autochtones/2009045/joliette-manawan-drone-medicament-hopital)). Chief Sipi Flamand and vice-chief Paul-Émile Ottawa supported it publicly; Ottawa cited the 2020 death of Joyce Echaquan at Joliette hospital, and the 86 km gravel road to Saint-Michel-des-Saints is described as dangerous ([Radio-Canada](https://ici.radio-canada.ca/espaces-autochtones/2009045/joliette-manawan-drone-medicament-hopital)). On Feb 12, 2024 the MEIE approved **up to $2 M non-refundable on a $4.55 M project**, accompanied by CRIAQ. Ecosystem partners are Thales, CAE, ASDR, Taïga Telecom, Drones Express and SCI Canada ([CRIAQ](https://www.criaq.aero/en/corridair/)). The official launch was May 2024 in Saint-Donat, alongside a CRIAQ social-acceptability study ([CRIAQ LinkedIn](https://fr.linkedin.com/posts/consortium-de-recherche-et-innovation-en-aerospatiale-au-quebec_lancement-du-projet-corridair-lann%C3%A9e-activity-7199047839086190593-OJUp)). Vertiko now describes it as "a 150 km BVLOS air corridor between Joliette hospital and Manawan" ([Vertiko network](https://vertikomobilite.ca/en/the-network/)).

The 2025 follow-ons point toward heavier aircraft. In July 2025, AIRO/Jaunt unveiled a "middle-mile" cargo drone carrying 250–500 lb over 200+ miles and cited CORRIDAIR as demonstrating potential "for First Nation communities in rural Quebec" ([AIRO](https://theairogroup.com/airo-introduces-middle-mile-cargo-drone-and-expansion-into-quebecs-ymx-innovation-zone-at-eaa-airventure-oshkosh/)). In Sept 2025, Vertiko took $2.5 M from Phoenix Capital B.V. for a 20% stake. It says it "has a cargo drone delivery program" and a consulting division "designing, building, and operating turnkey cargo … drone systems for third parties" ([Vertiko](https://vertikomobilite.ca/en/vertiko-mobility-receives-2-5-million-investment-from-phoenix-capital-b-v-another-step-towards-sustainable-advanced-aerial-mobility-aam-solutions/)). The project also framed operations as eventually "operated by trained community members" ([Vertiko EN](https://vertikomobilite.ca/en/press-release-air-mobility-for-the-atikamekw-community-of-manawan/)). **No published report of actual BVLOS flights, aircraft type or a 2026 delivery start was found.**

Compared with QxF7, the mission, community, health-system partner and route are close to identical: QxF7's Manawan → Saint-Michel-des-Saints leg is a sub-segment of CORRIDAIR's corridor. The aircraft class differs sharply. CORRIDAIR's industrial backers are oriented toward 250–500 lb cargo drones and eVTOL, while QxF7 is a 750 g specimen shuttle. CORRIDAIR also has funding, a CISSS partner and formal community consent, none of which QxF7 has. **Classification: potential partner, and the most direct conflict if ignored.** One plausible pitch, which is an inference, is QxF7 as a small-payload, low-cost electric aircraft for frequent specimen runs on Vertiko's corridor, a role its 250–500 lb platform does not cover.

### ECOGENE-21 / INNOVITECH and the Delair DT46: the closest aircraft concept, flown in France

ECOGENE-21 is a non-profit clinical research organisation coordinated from Saguenay–Lac-Saint-Jean and founded by Dr Daniel Gaudet of Université de Montréal. INNOVITECH is its innovation-management partner ([Newswire 2022](https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/ecogene-21-drones-to-promote-access-to-clinical-research-877783777.html)). Phase 1 **[pre-2024]**: on Sept 28, 2022 it ran clinical-trial delivery demonstrations at CED Alma with an ARA Robotique multirotor, flying medication to a mock pharmacy near Alma airport. The stated goal was routes such as Chicoutimi to rural Lac-Saint-Jean or Côte-Nord sites ([Radio-Canada 2022](https://ici.radio-canada.ca/nouvelle/1927811/transports-canada-pharmacie-region-eloignee)). That work was funded through Quebec's FACS (MEI, managed by CQDM), with the Montreal Heart Institute's clinical-trials coordination centre as a partner ([Newswire 2022](https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/ecogene-21-drones-to-promote-access-to-clinical-research-877783777.html)). Phase 2: on **Jan 16, 2024** a Delair DT46 flew more than 150 km carrying a temperature-controlled case of medications and blood samples ([Delair](https://delair.aero/press-releases/delair-dt46-drones-medicaux/)). It flew autonomous circles for about 3 h over the Lauragais plains at Labège, near Toulouse, **France**, after the Quebec team spent a week at Delair's test centre ([France 3 Occitanie](https://france3-regions.franceinfo.fr/occitanie/haute-garonne/toulouse/photos-un-drone-reussi-un-vol-de-livraison-de-medicaments-sur-plus-de-150-km-afin-d-aider-des-patients-isoles-2915880.html); [ECOGENE-21](https://ecogene21.org/en/news/vol-de-livraison-de-medicaments/)). The partners met through a Quebec mission run by Toulouse Métropole, Aerospace Valley and CRIAQ ([AeroMorning](https://aeromorning.com/le-drone-dt46-delair-teste-par-une-equipe-quebecoise-pour-la-livraison-longue-distance-de-medicaments-et-la-collecte-dechantillons-biologiques/)). No funder is named for the 2024 flight ([Delair PDF](https://delair.aero/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Communique-presse-DELAIR-30-janvier-2024.pdf)).

The DT46 is electric and configurable as VTOL or fixed-wing. It carries up to 5 kg payload with a 25 kg MTOW (VTOL), has 3 h 30 endurance in VTOL configuration and a 100 km communication range, and is fitted with a parachute and ADS-B/FLARM. Its operating temperature is **−10 °C to +50 °C** ([Delair datasheet](https://delair.aero/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Delair_EN_DT46_2023.pdf)). After the flight, ECOGENE-21 said it wanted to "identify partners (drone operators, other long-range users)" to build the first remote medical networks ([AeroMorning](https://aeromorning.com/le-drone-dt46-delair-teste-par-une-equipe-quebecoise-pour-la-livraison-longue-distance-de-medicaments-et-la-collecte-dechantillons-biologiques/); [Innovitech](https://innovitech.com/nos-realisations/test-pour-drones-et-livraison-de-medicaments/)). By 2025 its innovation page listed "UBI", a family of smart containers for transporting drugs, biological samples and gene-therapy vectors "by various means, including drone" ([ECOGENE-21](https://ecogene21.org/fr/nos-innovations/)). No Quebec flights, new funding or route announcements were found for 2025–26.

Compared with QxF7, the aircraft concept is closest: an electric fixed-wing VTOL with long range and a temperature-controlled payload. The DT46, however, is a 25 kg aircraft rated only to −10 °C, and the work was a demonstration, not a service. **Classification: partner (strongest mission partner).** It has health-research legitimacy, a drone-compatible container program, and a publicly stated need for a long-range drone partner.

### Drones Express: a short-hop multirotor precedent with uncertain status

Drones Express is based in the Quebec City area, with François Bertrand as CEO. In 2020 it planned 180 kg, gasoline-powered, 200 km-range drones for the Basse-Côte-Nord and signed a letter of intent with Drone Delivery Canada for Condor drones on Lower North Shore and Anticosti routes: a 10-year term worth $3.6 M to DDC **[pre-2024]** ([Radio-Canada 2020](https://ici.radio-canada.ca/nouvelle/1752307/drone-colis-livraison-basse-cote-kegaska); [Newswire 2020](https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/drone-delivery-canada-announces-letter-of-intent-with-drones-express-inc-for-condor-project-859284414.html)). No evidence was found that it launched; DDC later cancelled the Condor (see Rest of Canada). On Aug 29–30, 2024, Drones Express flew simulated medical parcels from the Baie-Saint-Paul hospital to L'Isle-aux-Coudres, about 6 km each way and "largely BVLOS", under an SFOC with MRC de Charlevoix approval ([CIMT/TVA](https://cimtchau.ca/nouvelles/transport-de-colis-medicaux-par-drone-des-demonstrations-concluantes-dans-charlevoix/); [CIHO](https://cihofm.com/news/livraison-de-colis-medicaux-par-drone-les-demonstrations-ont-ete-un-succes/)). The aircraft was an automated octocopter with an 11 kg payload ([CIHO](https://cihofm.com/news/drones-express-une-demonstration-technologique-des-juin-entre-lisle-aux-coudres-et-baie-saint-paul/)). The CIUSSS de la Capitale-Nationale "lacked resources" to proceed, and a promised fall-2024 pilot with an unnamed CIUSSS has no published outcome ([CIMT/TVA](https://cimtchau.ca/nouvelles/transport-de-colis-medicaux-par-drone-des-demonstrations-concluantes-dans-charlevoix/)). Its website now reads only "Notre site se refait une beauté" and lists a different contact ([dronesexpress.ca](https://dronesexpress.ca/)). Drones Express is a CORRIDAIR ecosystem partner.

Compared with QxF7, it shares the island-hospital medical-parcel framing and is the precedent for QxF7's Isle-aux-Grues test route, showing that an MRC will approve such demonstrations. Its aircraft is a heavy multirotor, not a long-range fixed-wing. Its stall shows that health-system adoption, not technology, is the bottleneck in Quebec. **Classification: neutral; possible source of lessons learned; status unknown.**

### Other Quebec medical-drone activity: urban, modelled or crewed

The MUHC and Héma-Québec flew simulated blood products onto the Montreal General Hospital roof in 2019 (9.8 km loops, up to 6.4 kg) **[pre-2024]** ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7899218/)). In Jan 2026, Héma-Québec's Danny Brouard said drones are "not on Héma-Québec's agenda" except possibly for mass-casualty events. He also noted that Quebec transport boxes must handle **−30 to +40 °C**, an independent validation of QxF7's thermal spec for the payload box ([Noovo](https://www.noovo.info/nouvelles/sante/article/des-drones-utilises-pour-le-transport-du-sang-bientot-une-realite-au-quebec/); [CityNews](https://montreal.citynews.ca/2026/01/25/drones-blood-transport-quebec/)). Concordia published an optimisation model of drone-assisted blood collection for Quebec City in Oct 2025, with no flights ([EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1103623?language=french)). Unither Bioélectronique in Bromont has evaluated drones since 2019 but now focuses on a crewed hydrogen-electric R44 for organ transport, targeting 370 km flights ([Vertical Mag](https://verticalmag.com/features/unither-expands-flight-envelope-of-hydrogen-electric-helicopter/)). Optilab grappes move specimens by contracted road transport with traceability. The SLSJ grappe, which also covers Côte-Nord and Nord-du-Québec labs, planned a dedicated transport tender. CIUSSS MCQ, which covers La Tuque, runs its server lab at CHAUR Trois-Rivières. No Optilab drone pilot was found ([CIUSSS SLSJ Optilab](https://santesaglac.gouv.qc.ca/medias/2023/11/Cahier_de_laboratoire_Optilab_-_vo_3_no_1.pdf); [CIUSSS MCQ](https://www.ciusssmcq.ca/communiques-de-presse/79/deploiement-du-projet-optilab-au-ciusss-mcq/)). None of these competes with QxF7. Héma-Québec's −30 °C box requirement and Optilab's contracting structure are the two facts that matter to its design and its sales pitch.

### Quebec long-range and cargo players: capable, but pointed elsewhere

**Volatus Aerospace**, which merged with Drone Delivery Canada, is moving its HQ to Mirabel and opened a 53,000 sq ft manufacturing facility there in June 2026. All of its medical routes are outside Quebec ([La Presse](https://www.lapresse.ca/affaires/entreprises/2024-11-15/volatus-se-pose-a-mirabel.php); [GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/08/13/3345013/0/en/volatus-aerospace-releases-q2-2026-financial-results.html)); it is profiled under Rest of Canada. **Laflamme Aéro** in Coleraine builds the LX300, an unmanned tandem-rotor heavy-fuel helicopter carrying up to 180 kg with about 10 h endurance. CED Alma is running operational tests ahead of entry into service in 2027 ([Transport Canada](https://tc.canada.ca/fr/apercus-tc/livraison-fournitures-voie-airs); [Le Lac-St-Jean](https://www.lelacstjean.com/actualites/8000000005237/le-ced-d-alma-se-prepare-a-l-ere-des-drones-autonomes)). **ARA Robotique** in Montreal builds multirotors (the ARA-405, about 15 kg) and the SKYMATE autopilot. It has about 40 staff, drones selling for $10–50k, and a production agreement with the Canadian Armed Forces, and it received $8.77 M from the MEIE in July 2025, together with Laflamme, for surveillance drones ([La Presse, Apr 2026](https://www.lapresse.ca/affaires/portfolio/2026-04-13/aerospatiale/des-drones-aux-interets-100-canadiens.php); [La Presse, Jul 2025](https://www.lapresse.ca/affaires/entreprises/2025-07-17/drones-de-surveillance/quebec-accorde-une-subvention-de-8-8-millions-a-deux-entreprises.php)). **Exo Drone** in Laval flies long-range VTOL fire surveillance for SOPFEU with ArgenTech (FireEye VTOL and Carbonix Volanti, "more than 10 h" claimed), which is surveillance rather than delivery ([Dronautique Québec](https://dronautiquequebec.com/etude-de-cas/exo_drone)). **Aeronyx** planned industrial delivery corridors at CED in 2021 **[pre-2024]**, with no recent flight evidence ([Radio-Canada 2021](https://ici.radio-canada.ca/nouvelle/1843966/technologie-alma-centre-excellence)). **Hydro-Québec/IREQ** and **Drone Volt** work on line inspection. **Innu Drone Inc.** of Maliotenam is Innu-owned and does mapping, not delivery ([innudrone.com](https://innudrone.com/)). None of them targets sub-1 kg medical runs.

### Quebec infrastructure and student players: the partner ring

**CED Alma (Centre d'excellence sur les drones)** manages more than 120,000 km² of VLOS/BVLOS zones near Alma, including restricted zone CYR 658 (4,276 km²), used mainly for BVLOS. Procedures developed with Transport Canada allow **authorisation in 5 business days**, and the site also has the Qualia test site, hangars and a mobile ground station ([CED BVLOS](https://www.cedquebec.ca/soutien-aux-operations/bvlos-vlos); [CED R&D](https://www.cedquebec.ca/soutien-et-innovation/recherche-et-developpement)). An $8.7 M "Horizon" investment (Aug 2024) bought test benches and ARA-405 drones so companies can test payloads "without having to invest in their own drone" ([CKAJ](https://ckaj.org/investissement-de-87-m-le-centre-dexcellence-sur-les-drones-dans-les-ligues-majeures/); [ARA](https://www.ara-uas.com/fr/latest-news)). In Oct 2026 it added ADS-B, TCAS/ACAS, detect-and-avoid and ground radar, and it also operates at Saint-Félicien ([Le Lac-St-Jean](https://www.lelacstjean.com/actualites/8000000005237/le-ced-d-alma-se-prepare-a-l-ere-des-drones-autonomes)). Its fees, and whether it offers a student or non-profit rate, are not published. **Espace Aéro**, Quebec's aerospace innovation zone, runs a "chantier dronautique", and its YMX hub at Mirabel hosts drone and AAM testing ([Espace Aéro](https://espaceaero.ca/chantier-dronautique/); [Mirabel](https://mirabel.ca/espace-aero)). **CRIAQ** sponsored CORRIDAIR and runs pre-competitive Exploration-Innovation calls, with a 2026 call open ([CRIAQ](https://www.criaq.aero/services/accompagner-et-acceder-au-financement/exploration-innovation/)).

Quebec student clubs all focus on multirotors or competitions, and no medical-delivery projects were found among them. ÉTS Dronolab placed 5th of 20 at AEAC 2026 ([LinkedIn](https://fr.linkedin.com/posts/dronolab_aeac-ets-studentengineering-activity-7468041076076019712-U9W5)). The McGill Robotics Drone Team, with about 40 members, made its first AEAC entry in 2026 with an **electric VTOL** ([GitHub](https://github.com/mcgill-robotics/drone-2026)). Polytechnique's Zenith placed 6th of 15 and 1st in Quebec at AEAC 2025 ([LinkedIn](https://fr.linkedin.com/posts/zenith-polytechnique-montr%C3%A9al_6e-sur-15-%C3%A9quipes-1er-au-qu%C3%A9bec-et-gagnant-activity-7330367479250116608-nE_N)). Sherbrooke's VAMUdeS is known as an AEAC competitor.

| CRIAQ / Espace Aéro | Partner (funding and convening) | Routes MEIE money; pre-competitive calls |
| Drones Express | Neutral / lessons | Multirotor, stalled on adoption |
| Volatus, Laflamme, ARA, Exo Drone | Not direct competitors | Heavier aircraft, surveillance or out-of-province medical work |
| ÉTS, McGill, Polytechnique, UdeS clubs | Partner (peers, recruits) | No medical projects; McGill already flies an electric VTOL |

## Rest of Canada: short multirotor hops, one VTOL research program, a trail of stalls

### Volatus Aerospace (ex-Drone Delivery Canada): the only recurring medical drone service in Canada

DDC acquired Volatus in an Aug 30, 2024 "merger of equals" and took the Volatus name ([Volatus MD&A](https://newsfile.futunn.com/public/NN-PersistNoticeAttachment/7781/20250501/SEDAR_PLUS/CSA_SEDAR_PLUS_NOTICE_RECORD_ID_2101463.pdf)). It trades on the TSX (FLT). Q2 2026 revenue was C$8.42 M, H1 2026 revenue C$14.05 M, and cash on hand was C$59.2 M. It now calls itself a "sovereign aerospace and defence platform" ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/08/13/3345013/0/en/volatus-aerospace-releases-q2-2026-financial-results.html)). Investissement Québec and EDC lent it C$15 M in Oct 2024. Its workhorse, the **Canary** multirotor, carries **4.5 kg over 20 km** at about 80 km/h with a parachute and was cold-tested at **−35 °C** ([DDC Q1 2024 MD&A](https://dronedeliverycanada.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Drone_MDA_Q1-2024.pdf)). One DDC spec page lists a 4 kg payload instead, a minor conflict ([DDC](https://dronedeliverycanada.com/landing-page/)). On July 8, 2026, Transport Canada issued a Letter of Acceptance under the new Pre-Validated Declaration process. Volatus describes it as the first for BVLOS over populated areas with onboard detect-and-avoid; it precedes, and is not yet, a final declaration ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/07/08/3323978/0/en/volatus-aerospace-advances-canada-s-next-chapter-in-autonomous-aviation.html)).

Two medical routes are active. The first runs from **Edmonton International Airport to Montana First Nation's Akamihk clinic**, 10.15 km along rail lines, launched in Sept 2024 and still active in April 2026, remotely piloted from Vaughan, ON ([YEG](https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/revolutionizing-shipping-drone-technology-soars-to-new-heights-at-yeg-822152073.html); [Skies Mag](https://skiesmag.com/features/volatus-aerospace-cutting-edge-bvlos/)). The second is **Halton Healthcare's Milton → Oakville** route, about 14 km, carrying blood and urine samples on demand with zero visual observers using ground-based radar ([Skies Mag](https://skiesmag.com/features/volatus-aerospace-cutting-edge-bvlos/)). Volatus reports more than 11,000 commercial operations and SFOCs that include "BVLOS northern and Arctic operations". Its longer-range options are a RigiTech Eiger fixed-wing VTOL (3 kg / 100 km, March 2025), a Dufour Aero2 (still in evaluation) and the Condor XL heavy helicopter (180 kg / 200 km, aimed first at offshore wind) ([UST](https://www.unmannedsystemstechnology.com/2025/03/volatus-expands-medical-delivery-network-with-long-range-drone/); [GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/01/27/3226378/0/en/Volatus-Advances-Dufour-Aerospace-Partnership-to-Develop-Runway-Independent-Cargo-Capabilities-for-Arctic-Defence-and-Commercial-Operations.html); [AirPro News](https://airpronews.com/2026/03/17/volatus-aerospace-secures-nrc-irap-funding-for-condor-xl-drone/)). No Eiger medical route was found operating in Canada. The original gasoline Condor was cancelled in 2023 after a total loss and a hard landing, with Volatus citing component reliability and "lack of regulatory progress for RPAs greater than 25 kg" ([DDC Q1 2024 MD&A](https://dronedeliverycanada.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Drone_MDA_Q1-2024.pdf)).

Compared with QxF7, Volatus does exactly the specimen work QxF7 targets, but over a range about 7× shorter, in road-connected southern settings, with a multirotor, from a central operations centre. The Canary's −35 °C test point is the coldest figure in the Canadian record, but it is a test result rather than a published operating limit, and no sustained far-north winter operation is documented. **Classification: incumbent; the most likely future competitor if it fields the Eiger on remote Quebec routes; also a possible aircraft or operations partner.** The Q2 2026 emphasis on defence suggests medical cargo is a showcase line rather than Volatus's revenue driver (inference from the notes).

### UBC Drone Transport Initiative + InDro Robotics: the closest Canadian mission analogue

Phase I (Oct 2021–Oct 2022) **[pre-2024]** flew a 5 km route between the Village of Fraser Lake and Stellat'en First Nation, about 100 km west of Prince George. It ran 14–16 flights per weekday, totalled **1,228 flights in 12 months**, used a DDC Sparrow, had Transport Canada BVLOS and Transport of Dangerous Goods approvals, and was funded by a **C$750,000 TD Ready Challenge** grant plus Canfor, LifeLabs and the Air Canada Foundation ([UBC DTI Phase I](https://dronetransport.med.ubc.ca/phase-i-2/); [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/british-columbia/stellaten-first-nation-ubc-fraser-lake-drone-delivery-medicines-1.6210216)). Lab transport was only process-mapped. A 2026 JMIR paper reports that once dangerous-goods approval arrived, "several community members received their medications by drone" ([JMIR](https://jopm.jmir.org/2026/1/e82720/PDF)). Phase II is an NFRF-funded, multi-year program led by PI Dr Femke Hoekstra. InDro will operate **fixed-wing VTOL** flights from **Prince George** to Fraser Lake, Stellat'en and three rural Indigenous communities in total, evaluating the **Sky-Drones SkyLane 350**, and is building weather and aircraft-detection stations along the route ([InDro](https://indrorobotics.ca/indro-part-of-new-rural-indigenous-healthcare-drone-delivery-project/)). The SkyLane 350 is electric, with a 35 kg MTOW, up to 10 kg payload, 2.0 h endurance at 10 kg, 22 m/s cruise and a rating of **−20 °C to +45 °C** ([Sky-Drones](https://sky-drones.com/skylane)). Phase 2 kicked off with a community event on July 23, 2026, and a UBC CPD webinar on Oct 14, 2026 will share "key findings" ([Stellat'en](https://www.stellaten.ca/drone-transport-initiative/); [UBC CPD](https://ubccpd.ca/learn/learning-activities/using-drone-technology-enhance-rural-and-remote-first-nations-healthcare)). No source confirms that Prince George → Fraser Lake has been flown. The team's own framing is that Canada has "yet to [see] a carefully thought-out long-range BVLOS system that reliably and consistently serves communities with pressing needs at scale."

Compared with QxF7, this is the same mission (remote Indigenous health, fixed-wing VTOL, hub-to-clinic), the same country and the same regulatory regime. It differs in using an aircraft about 10× heavier, rated only to −20 °C, and in being a well-funded, university-led research program rather than a student build. **Classification: partner, benchmark and mentor (UBC DTI).** The Oct 14 webinar is directly relevant.

### Stalls and failures: Moose Cree, COVID island routes, Condor, Ribbit

DDC's **Moose Cree / Moose Factory** program **[pre-2024]** began with an Oct 2017 agreement, ran a clean BVLOS pilot in Class E airspace in Sept 2018 carrying dry blood-spot kits, and signed a **C$2.5 M "year one revenue"** agreement in Dec 2018 that depended on Moose Cree securing federal funding. By Nov 2019 the community was still seeking C$2.1 M, and no source confirms that sustained service ever began ([DDC 2018](https://dronedeliverycanada.com/press-releases/drone-delivery-canada-signs-2-5-million-commercial-agreement-with-moose-cree-first-nation-to-deploy-ddcs-drone-delivery-platform/); [Northern Ontario Business](https://www.northernontariobusiness.com/industry-news/aboriginal-businesses/drones-taking-to-the-skies-in-moose-cree-first-nation-1885518)). The **Beausoleil and Georgina Island** COVID routes (2020) ran on six-month ISC proof-of-concept money and GlobalMedic funding, then ended ([DDC 2020](https://dronedeliverycanada.com/press-releases/drone-delivery-canada-announces-update-on-beausoleil-and-georgina-island-projects/)). **Ribbit**, founded in 2020 by Waterloo's Jeremy Wang and Carl Pigeon, retrofits light aircraft for autonomous northern cargo. It won C$387k from NRC-IRAP and a C$1.3 M Transport Canada/ISC contract, and holds letters of intent worth C$42 M/yr, but its commercial target has slipped from mid-2024 to "as soon as 2027", and it has repositioned as dual-use defence ([Open Canada](https://search.open.canada.ca/grants/record/nrc-cnrc,172-2020-2021-Q2-955100,current?amendments=); [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/cargo-airline-ribbit-signs-1-3m-contract-with-transport-canada-to-test-autonomous-flights-across-northern-canada-301870820.html); [Skies Mag](https://skiesmag.com/features/two-pilots-one-pilot-no-pilot-ribbits-plan-to-bring-pilotless-cargo-flights-to-canadas-north/); [Velocity](https://www.velocityincubator.com/news/pilotless-planes-for-the-hardest-to-reach-places)). Ribbit targets bulk cargo with a crewed-aircraft conversion, a different class from QxF7, so it is not a competitor; it shows how timelines slip. The **Foremost UAS Test Range** in Alberta, one of only two BVLOS-certified ranges in Canada (the other is Alma), closed on Sept 30, 2025 when its funding expired ([CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/calgary/foremost-drone-test-site-alberta-9.7003229)). That makes CED Alma scarcer and more valuable.

### Other Canadian players

**Sky Canoe** (Port Perry, ON) is Indigenous-controlled, with the Mississaugas of Scugog Island holding a controlling interest since 2023. It advertises the Nimkii 42-s (9.2 kg over 353 km) and a variant carrying 280 kg over 800 km, rated to "−35 °C", but no operating medical routes were found and all figures are manufacturer claims ([Sky Canoe](https://skycanoe.ca/); [INdurham](https://durham.insauga.com/mississaugas-of-scugog-invest-in-controlling-interest-of-sky-canoe-drone-company/)). **Indigenous Aerospace**, founded by Jacob Taylor of Curve Lake FN, partners with Volatus to train First Nations operators. Taylor is lead author of a peer-reviewed study on First Nations perspectives on health drones ([Indigenous Aerospace](https://indigenousaerospace.com/home-2/); [Drone Systems and Applications](https://cdnsciencepub.com/doi/10.1139/dsa-2024-0046)). That study is useful for QxF7's community-consent framing. **Draganfly** (Saskatoon) sells a multirotor Medical Response Drone with a temperature-managed box. Its medical work is in Ukraine and the US, and its Canadian Indigenous partnership (Ulkatcho FN) covers mapping and emergency response, not delivery ([Draganfly](https://draganfly.com/medical-response/); [Draganfly 2024](https://draganfly.com/press-release/draganfly-selected-as-an-exclusive-partner-of-the-ulkatcho-first-nations-ulkatcho-group-of-companies-to-provide-uav-solutions-services-training-public-safety-and-emergency-response-withi/)). **Avidrone** markets cargo UAS from 16 kg/100 km up to a 227 kg turbine helicopter, with no Canadian medical operations found ([Avidrone](https://avidrone.com/commercial/fleet/)). **ScriptRunner + Oak Medical Arts** used a DJI FlyCart 30 to fly insulin 30 km to Trout Lake from Thunder Bay in May 2024 as a one-off pilot ([SNNewsWatch](https://www.snnewswatch.com/local-news/new-drone-delivery-system-could-revolutionize-northern-healthcare-8800691)). **InDro**'s earlier trials (Salt Spring Island, 2019; Penelakut COVID tests, 2020–21) were short over-water hops **[pre-2024]**. Nunavut, NWT, Yukon, Labrador, Island Lake (MB) and northern Saskatchewan have no verifiable medical drone program. A widely circulated "SkyMeds" Yukon network article appears fabricated and should not be cited ([support-canadian.com](https://www.support-canadian.com/post/from-snowy-roads-to-skies-how-drones-are-redefining-prescription-access-in-canada)), and a claimed Beauval, SK route could not be verified.

**Student teams.** AEAC's **2020/2021** competition used a "remote Manitoba medication delivery" theme. Ryerson's RUAV won with a hybrid VTOL/fixed-wing; Sherbrooke's VAMUdeS placed 3rd, and ÉTS Dronolab was 3rd for its paper **[pre-2024]** ([AEAC 2021](https://www.aerialevolution.ca/canadas-national-student-uas-competition-2021-results/)). Waterloo WARG built "Icarus", a quadplane with 4 lift motors and a pusher, a 2.4 m span and an MTOW of 8 kg or less, the same layout as QxF7, and won the AEAC 2025 Phase 2 flight assessment ([WARG report](https://uwarg-docs.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/ARCHS22/pages/2159935555/Competition+Report); [WARG](https://www.uwarg.com/)). UBC UAS's VTOL Albatross placed 3rd in 2023–24 ([UBC UAS](https://ubcuas.com/projects)). No Canadian student team was found to have flown a long-range (about 100 km) medical VTOL operationally.

## Global commercial operators: Aviant is the template, and its trajectory is a warning

### Aviant (Norway): deep profile

**Origin.** Aviant was founded in 2020 by Lars Erik Fagernæs, Herman Øie Kolden and Bernhard Paus Græsdal, originally as "Norwegian Robotics", with NOK 125,000 from NTNU Discovery and TrønderEnergi and the rest self-funded ([NTNU](https://nyheter.ntnu.no/en/?p=9915)). The sources conflict on its origin. Press releases say "founded at MIT" ([Vertical Mag](https://verticalmag.com/press-releases/norwegian-drone-logistics-company-aviant-launches-kyte-receives-e1m-in-public-funding/)), while NTNU calls it an "NTNU startup" and a 2026 trade article says "three NTNU students" ([Logistikk Inside](https://www.logistikkinside.no/aviant-dronelevering-farmasiet/dronepilot-kan-endre-apoteklogistikken/1288043)). MIT Professional Education resolves this: the founders were **NTNU students who met at MIT in 2019** and pivoted an existing drone project to COVID-sample transport in 2020 ([MIT PE](https://professional.mit.edu/news/articles/developing-drones-address-pandemic-related-challenges-scandinavia)). A third-party claim that it was "founded in Oslo in 2018" contradicts every primary source and is unreliable ([Drone Delivery Grid](https://dronedeliverygrid.com/aviant-operator-profile/)). Fagernæs recalls: "The average age of the team was 24. We were going to fly 120 kilometres in civilian European airspace with blood samples … and we had no money" ([NTNU](https://nyheter.ntnu.no/en/ntnu-startup-receives-24-million-nok-from-the-research-council-of-norway/)). Kolden says they chose electric over gasoline deliberately, even though gasoline drones "can fly 10 times farther" ([MIT PE](https://professional.mit.edu/news/articles/flying-high-enable-sustainable-delivery-remote-care)).

**Aircraft.** The first 120 km+ hospital-to-hospital flight in early 2021 used a **DeltaQuad Pro**, a commercial electric quadplane, according to DeltaQuad's newsroom ([DeltaQuad, Mar 2021](https://www.deltaquad.com/newsroom/article/Aviant-performs-longest-ever-drone-flight-in-Norway/)). MIT's account instead says the prototypes were fixed-wing VTOLs built at MIT, and that the March 2021 proof of concept covered 120 km in 80 minutes ([MIT PE](https://professional.mit.edu/news/articles/developing-drones-address-pandemic-related-challenges-scandinavia)). Both may be true for different airframes, but the notes cannot resolve it. By Oct 2021 the fleet was a 2.6 m-span VTOL weighing about 13 kg with batteries, flying up to 100 km/h with about 160 km range and **about 1 kg payload**, partly sourced from a Chinese manufacturer with Aviant-built electronics **[pre-2024]** ([UAS Norway](https://www.uasnorway.no/aviant-henter-62-millioner-til-frakt-av-blodprover-med-drone/)). Later figures: up to 2 kg and 30 km each way (Feb 2024), up to 3 kg (Helsinki, Nov 2025), and **1.3 kg currently at Ski** (Jun 2026), working toward 2 kg. It has built 20 drones in total and is certified for 5 airborne at once but flies one at a time ([Unmanned Airspace](https://www.unmannedairspace.info/latest-news-and-information/aviant-launches-norways-longest-autonomous-home-drone-delivery-service-in-lillehammer/); [Helsinki](https://www.hel.fi/en/news/drones-deliver-medical-supplies-to-a-health-station); [Droneavisa](https://droneavisa.no/burger-pa-dora-med-drone-slik-reagerer-folk-pa-matlevering-fra-lufta/)). The company claims flights in winds up to 18 m/s and temperatures **as low as −26 °C**, and a longest flight of 124 km ([Aviant About](https://www.aviant.no/about); [aviant.no](https://www.aviant.no/)). A third-party registry instead lists −15 °C and 12 m/s ([Robotics Registry](https://roboticsregistry.net/companies/aviant)). The −26 °C figure is a demonstrated-flight claim, not a certified limit. Flight counts also differ: a Sep 2026 job ad says over 8,000, while the website says 9,000+ flights and 40,000+ km. No source names an aircraft "Kinguin", and no official MTOW or temperature spec sheet exists.

**Healthcare and winter record.** Aviant flew Trondheim–Røros (120 km) for **St. Olavs Hospital** in Feb 2021 and took over regular COVID-sample transport from Røros by the end of 2021 ([Aviant About](https://www.aviant.no/about)). Fagernæs says winter road closures made the drones "the only link" between test sites and labs (company claim) ([Dronewatch](https://www.dronewatch.eu/norwegian-drone-logistics-company-aviant-launches-kyte-receives-e1m-in-public-funding/)). The most useful dataset for QxF7 is the **Jämtland, Sweden** trial (Funäsdalen–Östersund, Nov–Dec 2021) **[pre-2024]**. Its last flight was 128 km in continuous snowfall. **Battery cooling was a problem below −20 °C** and was solved by insulating the batteries and limiting fuselage airflow. Airframe ice reduced endurance, and ground frost was removed with alcohol. The payload box stayed at or above +2.5 °C for 70 min, and sample quality matched road transport. However, **real patient samples were never flown**, because Östersund Hospital refused on privacy grounds ([Green Flyway/Aviant report](https://keep.eu/api/project-attachment/62575/get_file/)). The **Røros home-care pilot** (Oct–Dec 2024) made 46 flights over 25 days, while 38 flights were cancelled (31 before takeoff, 7 en route), mostly for winds of 13 m/s or more, fog and 4G problems ([Green Flyway 2025](https://greenflyway.no/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Drone-project-report2025.pdf)). That is a 45% cancellation rate, and it should calibrate QxF7's service-reliability promises. TINE flew dairy samples more than 60 km in winter 2025–26 ([Dfly](https://dfly.no/tine-tester-dronetransport/)). Aviant also flew a 7 km BVLOS medical-supplies route in Helsinki in Nov 2025, remote-piloted from Oslo ([Interreg CITYAM](https://interreg-baltic.eu/project-pilots/cityam/helsinki-pilot-2025/)).

**Pivot and return to health.** Kyte home delivery launched in 2023, followed by Lillehammer (17 km radius, 2024), Foodora Värmdö (2025; Aviant took over the contract after **Aerit went bankrupt**), and the first commercial base at Ski in April 2026, after which HQ moved from Trondheim to Ski ([Shifter](https://www.shifter.no/nyheter/droneselskapet-aviant-inngar-avtale-med-foodora/377604); [Logistikk Inside](https://www.logistikkinside.no/aviant-dronelevering-kyte/aviant-starter-kommersiell-dronelevering-i-ski/1249877)). In July 2026 the online pharmacy Farmasiet began a pilot with 10–15 min deliveries at a **NOK 40–80 freight price** ([Logistikk Inside](https://www.logistikkinside.no/aviant-dronelevering-farmasiet/dronepilot-kan-endre-apoteklogistikken/1288043)). Aviant stopped flying over cabin areas after complaints and estimates about 1 in 10 residents are negative. Health work is now returning through public programs. **Pilot Helse** received NOK 24 M from the Research Council of Norway (Feb 2026), with Brønnøysund lab samples, which currently travel by car once a day, as the example use case ([NTNU](https://nyheter.ntnu.no/en/ntnu-startup-receives-24-million-nok-from-the-research-council-of-norway/)). **Aero Aid** (2026–28) has Aviant as project owner with SINTEF, Norsk Luftambulanse Teknologi and four hospital trusts, covering four hospital-to-clinic corridors with a target of under 1 h for blood samples and **trials in 2027 and 2028** ([SINTEF](https://www.sintef.no/en/projects/2026/aero-aid-drones-more-effecient-norwegian-healthcare/)).

**Money and size.** Aviant raised NOK 6.2 M (2021), a €2.3 M seed led by Bring Ventures (2022), a €1 M Innovation Norway grant (2023), the US$1 M GENIUS NY prize (2024), and NOK 24 M for Pilot Helse (2026) ([Dronewatch](https://www.dronewatch.eu/norwegian-drone-logistics-company-aviant-launches-kyte-receives-e1m-in-public-funding/); [ESD](https://www.esd.ny.gov/esd-media-center/press-releases/empire-state-development-announces-round-eight-winners-genius-ny)). Sales were **NOK 4.8 M in 2025**, against costs of about NOK 17 M a year, and the operating loss narrowed to NOK 1.8 M. It has signed over NOK 30 M in contracts in six months, and a "large contract in the USA" was in negotiation in July 2026 with no confirmation since ([Shifter](https://www.shifter.no/nyheter/30-mill-i-kontrakter-og-usa-avtale-i-sikte-etter-ski-lansering/470044)). In Feb 2026 it had 22 employees, was hiring 6 more, and was valued near NOK 100 M ([NTNU](https://nyheter.ntnu.no/en/?p=9915)). No Canadian activity was found. MIT notes Aviant's interest in Europe and the US, and a third-party profile names Canada as a fit (analysis, not a company statement).

**Versus QxF7.** Aviant matches QxF7 on student origin, all-electric fixed-wing VTOL, Nordic winter, real hospital routes, and a 2021 envelope of about 1 kg at 120–160 km, close to QxF7's 750 g at 95 km. The differences matter more. Aviant became venture-funded within two years, uses a proprietary airframe, flies at 100 km/h, survives on consumer delivery, and has still not run routine scheduled patient-sample service at −30 °C. **Classification: role model and benchmark, and a long-term potential entrant to Canada.** Its path ran from NOK 125k university seed money to a pandemic-driven hospital champion, then commercial contracts, VC and large public R&D grants. It shows that the first hospital champion came before the money.

### Other commercial operators and aircraft vendors

**Zipline (USA)** runs the P1 fixed-wing (about 1.8 kg, 80 km service radius, 101 km/h, catapult launch, wire recovery, parachute drop) and the P2 (3.6 kg, 16 km radius, tethered droid), and has made more than 2 M deliveries to over 5,000 health facilities. In Jan 2026 it raised more than $600 M at a $7.6 B valuation ([Zipline](https://www.zipline.com/newsroom/zipline-surpasses-2-million-deliveries-raises-more-than-600m-to-power-next-phase-of-growth-and-expands-operations-to-houston-and-phoenix); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zipline_(drone_delivery_company))). It suspended three Ghana centres over about US$15 M in unpaid government bills, and its US-backed Africa expansion releases funds only when governments commit to cover operating costs ([BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/technology/article/u-s-backs-150m-zipline-expansion-to-transform-africas-healthcare-delivery/)). Cold testing reached only −5 °C icing, and it publishes no minimum temperature ([Zipline 2024](https://www.zipline.com/newsroom/the-wild-wild-nest-cold-weather-drone)). Its payload-and-range class is the same as QxF7's, but it operates at a scale QxF7 will never match and in a warm climate. **Wingcopter (Germany)** builds the W198 tilt-rotor (4.7 kg, 92–94 km, 90 km/h; **0 °C minimum** on its 2021 sheet, with no icing operation). It flew more than 2,150 medical flights in Malawi, which are now largely grounded after donor cuts. It has frozen the W198 configuration and moved into surveying and defence (W262, Sep 2026), raising about $108 M (PitchBook) or more than €120 M (FlightBrief) ([Wingcopter](https://wingcopter.com/wingcopter-198); [spec 2021](https://www.sisirl.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/Specsheet_Wingcopter-198.pdf); [D+C](https://www.dandc.eu/en/article/malawi-medical-drones-once-stregthened-healthcare-funding%20cuts); [FlightBrief](https://flightbrief.news/articles/wingcopter-262-defence-pivot-ukraine/)). **Avy (Netherlands)** makes the Aera, which has **the same 4-lift + 1-pusher quadplane layout as QxF7**: 19.5 kg MTOW, 1 kWh battery, 3 kg payload (2 kg with parachute), 100 km standard range, 83 km/h cruise, heated pitots, and a MedBox holding 2–8 °C for about 7 h. It holds a Dutch BVLOS LUC for medical corridors and claims "extreme cold" with no number ([Avy](https://avy.eu/product/aera); [Coptrz](https://coptrz.com/shop/drones/avy-aera/)). **DeltaQuad (Netherlands)** sold the Pro #CARGO Aviant first used: 6.2 kg MTOW, 1.2 kg payload, **100 km at 1 kg**, 60–65 km/h, **−20 °C**, from **€9,999** at a reseller ([Equator Drone](https://www.equatordrone.com/deltaquad-pro-view/); [DeltaQuad manual](https://docs.deltaquad.com/deltaquad-pro-operations-manual-v2/vehicle-specifications)). The newer Evo is marketed for surveillance. **Rigitech Eiger II**, flown by **Delivrone**, flew 80 km hospital routes in Normandy in 2025 at 11–35 °C. Its limit excludes temperatures below −7 °C, and the peer-reviewed authors conclude long-range drone transport "cannot be considered reliable without substantial backup solutions" ([MDPI Drones](https://www.mdpi.com/2504-446X/10/1/71)).

Urban hospital operators are a different product. **Apian**, an NHS integrator using Wing and Matternet, runs 3-minute pathology hops claimed to be "up to 23% cheaper than existing urgent couriers" ([UST](https://www.unmannedsystemstechnology.com/2026/07/autonomous-cargo-drone-network-accelerates-nhs-pathology-logistics/)). **Matternet** has an FAA type-certified M2 (2 kg, 20 km) but reported FY2025 revenue of $392,739 and a going-concern warning ([FlightBrief](https://flightbrief.news/articles/matternet-going-concern-sec-filings-2026/)). **Everdrone** runs AED multicopters within an 8 km radius for more than 300,000 Swedish residents. **Skyports** operates Royal Mail Orkney and NHS Wales trials, calling unsegregated BVLOS airspace "the key challenge" ([Vertus](https://vertus.aero/skyports-drone-services-conducts-flight-tests-for-8220project-heart-of-the-dragon8221-at-snowdonia-a)). **Swoop Aero** (Australia) made 1.6 M deliveries yet went into liquidation in Nov 2024, citing under-capitalisation and an inability to raise "a few hundred thousand dollars" to finish its Kite aircraft ([Startup Daily](https://www.startupdaily.net/topic/business/drone-delivery-startup-swoop-aero-lands-in-liquidation/)). **Skyfarer** (UK) is listed as out of business in Aug 2026 by PitchBook alone, and **Aerit** (Sweden) went bankrupt. **Falck** flew Greenland health routes of 75–130 km in 2022 on a **hybrid fuel/battery** aircraft, chosen because "Arctic weather drains batteries extremely quickly" and rated to −20 °C, with no follow-up found **[pre-2024]** ([High North News](https://en.highnorthnews.com/science/testing-drones-in-arctic-health-care/200610)). **Elroy Air**'s Chaparral (hybrid, 227 kg+) and **Windracers ULTRA** (fuel, about 90 kg) are heavy cargo aircraft with no overlap with QxF7. **Jedsy** (Swiss drone-glider, Asklepios lab-sample operations) is supported only by headlines.

## Global non-commercial, student and open-source efforts: low-cost builds never reached service

**"Print, Build, Fly, Heal" in Alamos, Sonora, Mexico (2025)** is the closest analogue on cost and construction. Volunteers moved from hobby planes to locally built, **3D-printed autonomous planes** for urgent medical supplies to mountain communities, with a prototype costing **about US$1,000** and a plan to halve that. It is organised as a club with local medical students, with plans for a "lean nonprofit". The team names organisational ownership, not technology, as its biggest challenge, and it reports no flight results, payload or range ([HOPE_16 listing](https://store.2600.com/products/hope_16-2025-print-build-fly-heal-3d-printed-autonomous-planes-in-rural-mexico-download)). Its cost model and non-profit structure resemble QxF7's, but it operates in a hot climate and is unproven. **Virginia Tech EcoSoar** in Malawi (2017) had 13 local students build foam-and-3D-printed aircraft in two days, one of which flew a 19 km autonomous simulated delivery **[pre-2024]** ([UNICEF](https://www.unicef.org/innovation/drones/students-built-drone-medicine-delivery-malawi)). It grew into ADDA, which has 1,109 graduates in 31 countries but publishes no delivery-mission data ([ADDA](https://www.adda.me.vt.edu/)). **Kenyatta University's Luna Drone System** has 18 students designing a hybrid VTOL fixed-wing with a Peltier-cooled biomedical bay, partnered with the Kenya Red Cross and KEMSA. As of April 2026 nothing had flown and no payload or range was specified ([EPICS in IEEE](https://epics.ieee.org/blogs/epics-in-ieee-healthcare-access-flies-high-in-kenya/)). **Project Kestrel** (Sheffield) is a 50+ member student VTOL project aimed at East African medical supply, which pivoted to the IMechE competition with no deliveries ([PCBWay](https://www.pcbway.com/project/sponsor/Project_Kestrel_f8b704cf.html)). **Ohio State's STUART** is a 120 lb student VTOL rescue testbed that has made only confirmation flights ([OSU](https://engineering.osu.edu/news/2026/07/these-drone-builders-are-reshaping-emergency-rescue)).

**Flightory** sells Stork STL/STEP files for $29.99 plus a $14.99 VTOL Pack, and offers the Stallion, Super Stingray and Moose. The founder is unnamed, and no documented medical or humanitarian use of any Flightory airframe was found ([flightory.com](https://flightory.com/); [Flightory Our Story](https://flightory.com/?p=1126)). The license restricts use to "personal, non-commercial purposes", and "commercial or institutional use … is strictly prohibited", covering companies, organizations, schools, universities, research institutions and built aircraft. Modifications are allowed for personal use only, and a commercial license is available by contacting Flightory ([Flightory License](https://flightory.com/license/)). Non-profits are not named explicitly but appear to fall under "organizations". A student project formally run through a school for a teacher-graded report may also count as institutional use (inference). The open-source community offers no validated cold-weather or long-range data. The **Eclipson** E-VTOL is a tailsitter with no delivery use, and **WeRobotics** 3D-printed only add-on modules, not airframes ([Sculpteo](https://www.sculpteo.com/blog/2021/06/24/werobotics-use-additive-manufacturing-to-be-innovative-and-adaptive/)).

**Institutional programs proved the concept repeatedly and almost never became services.** **UAF ACUASI** in Alaska flew a 1,000 km, 8 h 47 min circuit on Aug 19, 2026 carrying about 200 lb of blood in temperature-controlled boxes, with no change against ground controls ([Alaska's News Source](https://www.alaskasnewssource.com/2026/09/24/this-could-be-life-saving-uaf-drone-program-tests-long-distance-blood-transport/)). The notes conflict on the aircraft. The commercial-landscape note cites the UAF Geophysical Institute as naming the fuel-powered **Windracers ULTRA** ([UAF GI](https://www.gi.alaska.edu/news/uaf-drone-program-tests-long-distance-blood-sample-delivery)). The non-commercial note could not fetch the UAF page and only infers the ULTRA from its 200 lb capacity. ACUASI also made a parachute medical drop from the ULTRA at **−18 °F (about −28 °C)**, likely in Jan 2026 (date inferred) ([UAF GI](https://www.gi.alaska.edu/news/acuasi-has-successful-parachute-delivery-test-new-drone)). Alaska's 2026 Rural Health Transformation awards include $250K for Tanana Chiefs Conference to plan drone pharmacy delivery to 42 villages where winter delays run 5–14 days ([CMS](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/press-releases/trump-administration-announces-160-million-bring-cutting-edge-technology-drones-prescription)). **Project CAELUS** (Scotland) had 16 partners and £8.5 M, flew Edinburgh → Borders General (about 30 miles, just over 30 min, compared with up to 5 h by road) under a CAA corridor in 2024, and closed with **no permanent service** ([Strathclyde](https://www.strath.ac.uk/whystrathclyde/news/2025/projectshowspotentialoffirstnationalmedicaldronesnetworkforscotland/); [Catapult](https://cp.catapult.org.uk/article/medical-drone-delivery-trial-takes-to-the-skies-over-scotland/)). **WeRobotics/Flying Labs** pursue "locally-led, locally-owned" cargo drones. Nepal's DrOTS cut TB sputum pickups from 2–3 days to about 25 minutes in 2019, but its funding ran only through December 2019 **[pre-2024]** ([Nepali Times](https://nepalitimes.com/nepal-tests-and-treats-tb-with-a-flying-pharmacy); [WeRobotics](https://werobotics.org/blog/our-strategy-on-cargo-drone-projects-for-public-health)). Norway's defence research agency, **FFI**, judged in 2022 that hospital blood-sample drones are realistic only with on-board ice protection and weather-aware planning ([FFI](https://www.ffi.no/en/publications-archive/icing-and-wind-implications-and-mitigations-in-high-intensity-safety-critical-drone-operations-in-norway)).

**Ubiq Aerospace**, an NTNU spin-off, holds the leading icing solution. D•ICE uses electrothermal carbon-fibre panels on wings and propellers and infers ice from aerodynamic and thermal anomalies; it was demonstrated at Arctic Warrior 2025 but targets defence and larger UAVs ([IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/amp/ubiq-aerospace-brings-the-first-drone-deicing-system-to-market-2650279632); [Ubiq](https://ubiqaerospace.com/tech/d-ice-systems-demonstrated-at-arctic-warrior-experiment-2025)). NTNU simulation found a 0.533 m propeller's **thrust turned negative after about 162 s** in continuous-maximum icing at −5 °C ([Müller & Hann 2023](https://folk.ntnu.no/richahan/Publications/2023_M%c3%bcller_SAE_postprint.pdf)). QxF7's 7–10-inch props would plausibly ice faster, though that is an untested extrapolation.

## Where QxF7 is genuinely differentiated, and where the claim is weak

**Genuine: the niche itself is unoccupied.** No source found documents a routine, scheduled, same-day lab-specimen service of 50–100 km to nurse-run clinics in sub-−20 °C conditions, anywhere. In Canada, operating medical drone routes are 10–14 km, and the one fixed-wing VTOL research program is just starting with an aircraft rated to −20 °C ([InDro](https://indrorobotics.ca/indro-part-of-new-rural-indigenous-healthcare-drone-delivery-project/)). Quebec has a funded but unflown corridor and stalled demonstrations. The specimen focus is also well chosen. Wemotaci's fixed weekly blood-draw day, Optilab's centralised lab chain, and Brønnøysund's once-a-day car run in Norway all point to a scheduled, predictable payload that suits a small fixed-wing better than on-demand multirotors ([Wemotaci](https://www.wemotaci.ca/sante); [NTNU](https://nyheter.ntnu.no/en/ntnu-startup-receives-24-million-nok-from-the-research-council-of-norway/)).

**Genuine if proven, and the biggest technical risk: −30 °C electric.** No electric fixed-wing VTOL found publishes an operating limit below −20 °C (DeltaQuad), and most sit at −12 °C, −7 °C or 0 °C. Aviant's −26 °C is a flight claim. Aviant's own Jämtland report says batteries struggled below −20 °C until insulated ([Green Flyway](https://keep.eu/api/project-attachment/62575/get_file/)). The peer-reviewed Li-ion UAV study saw failure below about −25 °C ([Li et al.](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/energy/v219y2021ics0360544220325883.html)). Greenland and Alaska both chose fuel or hybrid aircraft specifically to avoid cold-battery loss. Icing in the 0 to −15 °C band is likely a larger practical limiter than dry cold. Logged winter data would itself be a differentiator. The Government of Canada's Aug 2026 Arctic UAS call, which asks for −40 to +40 °C, MTOW of 25 kg or less, VTOL preferred and domestic production, shows the demand is real ([CanadaBuys via Govly](https://app.govly.com/public/opportunities/16965631)). Until the aircraft flies cold, though, the −30 °C figure is a target, not a capability.

**Weak: "low cost".** The CA$1,700–3,550 parts estimate is the team's own and unverified. The comparison point may also be overstated: the only price in the notes for the DeltaQuad Pro #CARGO, whose envelope nearly matches QxF7's (1.2 kg payload, 100 km at 1 kg, −20 °C), is a reseller's **"starting at €9,999"** ([Equator Drone](https://www.equatordrone.com/deltaquad-pro-view/)). The US$12–17k figure may reflect a configured kit, but no source in the notes supports it, so the report should cite the €9,999 starting price or source its own figure. More importantly, the airframe is not where the cost sits. Matternet's M3 redesign aims to remove the on-site person because labour is the key cost driver ([DroneXL](https://dronexl.co/2026/09/28/matternet-m3-drone-delivery-platform-2027/)). Recurring costs include ops staff at both ends, a Level 1 Complex pilot, an RPOC with maintenance and safety programs, a C$1,200 PVD fee, Standard 922 compliance evidence, insurance, dangerous-goods approval, and a Flightory commercial license. 3D printing addresses Swoop Aero's capital-intensity failure but not these ([Startup Daily](https://www.startupdaily.net/topic/business/drone-delivery-startup-swoop-aero-lands-in-liquidation/)). The real comparison is cost per delivery against the incumbent courier or charter. That figure is unpublished for every long-range medical operator, though Jämtland's 2021 road-courier baseline of SEK 750/day gives one anchor ([Green Flyway](https://keep.eu/api/project-attachment/62575/get_file/)).

**Weak: "small remote communities" as a differentiator in Quebec.** CORRIDAIR already serves the identical framing on the identical Manawan route, with formal community consent. UBC DTI does the same in BC. The differentiator is the aircraft class (sub-1 kg, cheap, frequent), not the target population.

**Weak: "non-profit".** The record shows non-profit and grant-funded medical drone programs as the most failure-prone model: Moose Cree's funding never closed, the COVID routes ended with emergency money, Nepal's funding ended in Dec 2019, Malawi was grounded after donor cuts, and CAELUS closed without a service. Durable services had a committed government payer or a high-volume commercial side business ([D+C](https://www.dandc.eu/en/article/malawi-medical-drones-once-stregthened-healthcare-funding%20cuts); [Strathclyde](https://www.strath.ac.uk/whystrathclyde/news/2025/projectshowspotentialoffirstnationalmedicaldronesnetworkforscotland/)). Even Aviant survives on burgers and public R&D grants. Non-profit status may help with grants and community trust, but it does not solve steady-state funding, and Quebec drone money has gone overwhelmingly to companies and consortia.

**Neutral to weak: speed and payload.** QxF7's 70–80 km/h cruise is below Aviant's and Zipline's roughly 100 km/h and Avy's 83 km/h, and above the stock Stork's 50–70 km/h. Range is prioritised, which is defensible, but a 91.5 km Wemotaci → La Tuque leg against a northern headwind at −30 °C leaves little margin on a 95 km design range.

## Implications and suggested next steps

The strategic picture favours positioning QxF7 as a **low-cost, cold-validated, small-payload aircraft and dataset that slots into others' programs**, rather than as a standalone service competing for routes. Concretely:

**Regulatory path.** Since **Nov 4, 2025**, lower-risk BVLOS needs no SFOC if four conditions hold: the pilot holds a **Level 1 Complex** certificate (minimum age 18, Advanced exam, 20 h ground school, Level 1 Complex exam, flight review, C$125); the operator holds an **RPOC**, with an accountable executive, a maintenance lead, a training program, SOPs and safety risk management; the aircraft has a **Standard 922** safety assurance declaration; and the flight stays in uncontrolled airspace, at or below 400 ft AGL, **more than 9.3 km (5 NM) from listed aerodromes** ([TC Level 1 Complex](https://tc.canada.ca/en/aviation/drone-safety/learn-rules-you-fly-your-drone/drone-operation-categories-pilot-certificates/level-1-complex-operations); [TC RPOC](https://tc.canada.ca/en/aviation/drone-safety/drone-pilot-licensing/apply-rpas-operator-certificate-rpoc)). Over unpopulated land (more than 1 km from any area above 5 people/km²) a self-declaration suffices. Over sparsely populated areas (5–25 people/km²), a small RPA needs a **Pre-Validated Declaration** (C$1,200), including 922.07 reliability and 922.12 environmental envelope, with the final declaration due within two years of acceptance ([TC summary](https://tc.canada.ca/en/aviation/drone-safety/2025-summary-changes-canada-drone-regulations); [TC PVD](https://tc.canada.ca/en/aviation/drone-safety/submitting-drone-safety-assurance-declaration/apply-pre-validated-declaration)). **La Tuque, Saint-Michel-des-Saints and Montmagny all have aerodromes**, so clinic-end landing sites within 9.3 km of them, plus the final approach into any village, will likely need either a PVD or an SFOC, and must be checked route by route. The −30 °C claim will eventually have to be demonstrated under 922.12, and the notes did not find how that must be shown. UBC DTI also needed separate **Transport of Dangerous Goods** approval for medications and specimens ([UBC DTI](https://dronetransport.med.ubc.ca/phase-i-2/)). At least one team member must be 18 or older to act as pilot.

Transport Canada guidance on carrying UN3373 Category B specimens by RPAS under the post-2025 rules. How Standard 922.12 requires a cold-weather envelope to be demonstrated. Whether UBC DTI Phase II has flown Prince George → Fraser Lake. Aviant's certified temperature limit (−26 °C claimed versus −15 °C third-party), whether its first 2021 flight used a DeltaQuad Pro or an MIT-built prototype, its true flight count (8,000+ versus 9,000+), and whether its US contract closed. The aircraft used for ACUASI's Aug 2026 blood flight (named as the ULTRA in one note, inferred in the other) and the year of its −28 °C drop. The AEAC 2027 theme, which the primary AEAC page gives as wildlife survey while a June 2026 DroneXL article said it was unannounced. Wingcopter's claimed student-project origin. Wingcopter's current temperature limit after 2021. Zipline's and Avy's numeric cold limits. Sky Canoe's and Avidrone's real operating status. Per-delivery costs for any long-range medical route. Whether QxF7's 95 km figure assumes recharging at the destination clinic, which would add ground infrastructure. This last item is a design question raised by this review, not by the notes. Low-reliability sources excluded from evidence: the Soleica Nunavik blog, the support-canadian.com "SkyMeds" article, the Devpost "Aero'ed" charter-cost figure (C$5,000–15,000 per medication delivery to James Bay), and the Beauval, SK route.

## Conclusion

The research changes QxF7's story from "first mover on remote Quebec medical drones" to "the missing small, cold-proven aircraft in an ecosystem that already has corridors, consortia and stalled demonstrations." Quebec has political consent (Manawan), money (MEIE, FACS, CRIAQ), test airspace (Alma), and a clinical partner looking for exactly this kind of aircraft (ECOGENE-21). What it lacks is a cheap electric fixed-wing VTOL with logged −30 °C flights and a payer for scheduled runs. Those two gaps, cold-weather evidence and a steady-state payer, are QxF7's real product, and both matter more than the airframe price.

The highest-leverage near-term moves cost nothing to build. They are a Flightory license conversation, a call to Vertiko and the Manawan council before announcing routes, and a winter test campaign at CED Alma designed from the start to produce Standard 922.12 evidence. Aviant's trajectory shows the downside: a team that wins on technology can still drift to burger delivery when no health system budgets for the service. QxF7's non-profit framing is only an asset if a Santé Québec establishment or Optilab grappe is named as the eventual payer from day one.
